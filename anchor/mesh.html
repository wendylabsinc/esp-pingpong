Drag a board to move it, double-click it to remove it; Esc lets go of a drag. A dashed ring is where a board or a sound really is. Dots, crosses and distances are what the Anchor worked out from simulated microphone audio alone, so a board that was just added or moved has only its ring until three ranging rounds agree on its distances.

A solid line is a distance that at least three recent rounds agree on, and those lines are drawn to scale. A dashed line is the best guess so far. A faded line has had no new round for 30 seconds. Dotted lines join the anchor to the boards its radio hears; it measures no distance to them. Sound gives distances only, so the picture may be turned or mirrored against the room. A sound heard by three or more boards is drawn where its arrival times put it, with the region it is 95% likely to be in. An orange arrow joins two heard sweeps when the second came within 12 seconds, somewhere else, and near enough to have been walked: something that moved. The band around the arrow is where its path could lie, as wide at each end as that sound's region, strongest in the middle.
